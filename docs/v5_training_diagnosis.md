# v5 training collapse: diagnosis and corrective design

Status: diagnosis complete; fixes proposed, not yet implemented. Revision 12 (final) — incorporates eleven rounds of adversarial review (three independent analyzer passes per round); every adopted reviewer claim was verified by fresh reads/computations or labeled pure deduction before inclusion, and the checkpoint rank-IC, censorship, sigma-floor, and price-floor measurements were re-run/replicated across rounds to adjudicate reviewer disagreements.
Scope: why both v5 training runs produce their best validation loss at the first evaluation, never beat the input-ignoring baseline, and degrade afterwards — and what to change. All numbers are measured from this repository's logs, data, and checkpoints; the few items resting on inference rather than direct measurement are labeled as such where they appear.

---

## 1. Definitions

- **z-label** `z[t,h] = ln(C[t+d_h]/C[t]) / (sigma_hat[t] * sqrt(d_h))` — the volatility-normalized forward log-return; `d_h ∈ {1, 5, 21, 126}` trading days; `sigma_hat` is the causal RiskMetrics EWMA (`features_v5.ewma_sigma_hat`).
- **Loss (CE)** `v5_backbone.v5_loss`: masked cross-entropy of a 60-bin histogram against HL-Gauss soft targets, plus `0.1 ×` a 3-class {down, neutral, up} NLL term at threshold `theta = 5 * 0.1348 = 0.674` on the z axis. The 3-class probabilities are partial sums of the same histogram, so the two terms share one predictor.
- **Uniform level**: CE of the uniform histogram = `ln 60 + 0.1 × 1.407 = 4.235`. (The class term is coupled to the histogram: a uniform histogram implies class probabilities 25/60, 10/60, 25/60, giving 3-class NLL 1.407 at the realized rates 0.21/0.58/0.21 — not `ln 3`.)
- **Marginal baseline** (`run_train_v5.val_marginal_baseline_ce`): CE of the best *input-ignoring* predictor on the validation fold (mean soft target + marginal class rates). Run-2 value: **3.5478**. Note the flavor: this constant is fit on the val fold itself (a val-oracle), which is strictly *harder* to beat than the honest reference (train marginal scored on val); the difference is ~1e-3–1e-4 nats here (train/val class rates differ by ~0.01), but since observed miss margins run as small as 0.006 nats, the next run should log both flavors. A model at this level has learned the unconditional distribution and nothing conditional. Cross-check: for z ≈ N(0,1), discretized entropy ≈ `1.4189 − ln(0.1348) = 3.423`, + HL-Gauss smoothing ≈ 3.45, + `0.1 × 0.971` (class entropy at rates 0.21/0.58/0.21) ≈ 3.55. The z marginal is therefore approximately unit normal, as the vol-normalization intends; all observed levels are internally consistent.
- **Rank-IC**: Spearman correlation, across tickers on one date, between a model score and the realized `z` for that horizon. The cross-sectional analogue of information coefficient. Score caveat: the probes below use `Score = P(up) − P(down)`, but both shipped consumers rank by raw `P(up)` (their `STD_FACTOR`/`--std-factor` defaults are 0, making `adj_prob ≡ P(up)`), and neither score is a monotone transform of the other when predicted neutral mass varies cross-sectionally — S3 therefore logs the IC of **both** scores, and the consumers should move to the already-emitted `Score_{h}` columns (or the gated score must match the consumed one). Reference scale: genuine equity cross-sectional signals sustain IC ≈ 0.02–0.05; 0.10 is exceptional. Single-date ICs disperse by ±0.1–0.2, so few-date probes are unreliable — every IC quoted below states its date count.

## 2. Observed failure

Run 2, same shape across all 4 members:

| eval step | epoch | val CE (member 0) | train CE (EMA, dropout on) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | 0.11 | **3.5569** (best; baseline 3.5478 → MISS) | 3.3219 |
| 30,000 | 0.33 | 3.7154 | 3.1426 |
| 50,000 | 0.55 | 3.8073 | 3.0778 |
| 90,000 | 1.00 | 3.9582 | 3.0105 |
| 110,000 | 1.22 | 4.0449 (early stop) | 2.9671 |

- Best val CE occurs at the **first** evaluation for every member in both runs.
- No member ever beats the marginal baseline (best values 3.5540–3.5755 vs 3.5478). Caveat: this was only ever tested per member. The product ships the 4-member ensemble mean, whose val CE is ≤ the mean member CE (Jensen); with miss margins of 0.006–0.028 nats a flip is conceivable though unlikely (member errors are strongly correlated). The ensemble-level number should be computed at the next finalize — it has never been measured.
- The train-CE EMA falls below the baseline between steps 200 and 300 (3.6654 → 3.5418; the EMA lags, so the underlying crossing is earlier still) — inside the 1,000-step warmup — and sits 0.58 nats below it by step 110k, with dropout active, so the true train CE is lower still.
- Val CE rises +0.49 nats over 100k steps. The rise is not strictly monotone (member 0 dips 3.9909 → 3.9582 at steps 80k → 90k; member 3 has two such dips) but every excursion stays far above the first-eval value.
- Run 1 shows the same shape at different levels: best 4.09–4.17 — between the marginal level of its 2003–2013 val slice (≈ 3.54, computed post hoc; not logged by that run) and the uniform level 4.235 — rising to 5.26 by epoch 3.

Interpretation constraint: the model extracts a large amount of *train-era conditional structure* almost immediately, and **none of it transfers in CE terms** — no member's val CE ever reaches the input-ignoring level; past the marginal point the conditioning actively damages val CE (confident, wrong conditionals cost more than ignorance). Post-hoc *ranking* probes of the saved checkpoint are more equivocal at short/mid horizons (§3.3): the CE-judged protocol had no metric that could have detected transfer in the form the product consumes, which is D3's point.

## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 Per-date common component of the labels

Method: 706-ticker even subsample of the universe; per ticker compute `z` via the repo's own `ewma_sigma_hat` / `_targets`; keep **spike-free labels only** (the trained-label population — without this filter the R² comes out roughly half, because unmasked split prints inflate the idiosyncratic variance); per (date, horizon), over dates with ≥ 30 names, compute the cross-sectional mean and the variance decomposition `R² = 1 − Var(z − mean_date(z)) / Var(z)`. Gaussian-equivalent CE headroom of knowing the date mean exactly: `−0.5 ln(1 − R²)`.

| horizon | train R² (nats) | val R² (nats) |
|---|---|---|
| 1d  | 0.150 (0.081) | 0.159 (0.087) |
| 1w  | 0.172 (0.094) | 0.187 (0.104) |
| 1m  | 0.208 (0.117) | 0.133 (0.072) |
| 6m  | 0.209 (0.117) | 0.086 (0.045) |

Replication status (twelve independent subsamples, 223–317 tickers, eight review rounds): the **train** column reproduces within ±0.01–0.02 at 1d/1w and ±0.03 at 1m/6m (observed train-6m range 0.184–0.236); the **val** column varies by ±0.03–0.05 at 1m/6m across subsamples (a single 254-session year of overlapping long-horizon labels has few effective observations — see §6), so read the 1m/6m entries as indicative, not precise. Estimator note: fitting one mean per date mechanically absorbs ≈ (1/n̄)(1−R²) of residual variance — an upward bias of ≈ +0.005–0.010 absolute at these cross-section sizes, inside the bands above and immaterial to the 15–21% conclusion, but systematic.

15–21% of label variance is a **shared per-date shift** (the market component; vol-normalization rescales it but does not remove it). Properties:

- On train dates it is *memorizable*: one smooth curve per horizon over the 15,859 train sessions (9,990 of them measurable with ≥ 30 names in the probe subsample), trivial for a 6.15M-parameter network.
- On future dates it is unforecastable at any signal size this pipeline could detect (market timing; weak aggregate-return predictability may exist in principle at magnitudes far below this design's floor), so its memorization transfers effectively zero.
- Memorizing it alone is worth ≈ 0.08–0.12 nats per horizon of train CE — about half of the observed 0.23-nat train advantage at step 10k, before counting date-conditional width/skew and sector-level date structure.

### 3.2 The inputs identify the date — but era identity is overdetermined

- `fg` is a single **global** series (CNN Fear & Greed, coverage 2011-01-03 → 2026-03-13) joined to every ticker by date. Every 252-row window dated in the fg era (55.2% of run-2 training rows; slightly more of training windows, whose anchors start at each ticker's 126th row) contains a 252-sample excerpt of one global curve — a unique fingerprint of the anchor date. `fg_corr` couples each ticker to the same curve.
- The calendar channels give position-in-year/quarter; market-wide volatility signatures (2008, 2020, …) identify the era coarsely; and the raw 252-day price/volume path itself fingerprints (ticker, era) with no auxiliary channel at all.
- Control observation: run 1's training era (1962 → 2003) predates fg coverage entirely, yet run 1 collapsed with the identical shape. Date/era identification is therefore **overdetermined** — fg is the cleanest key, not a necessary one. Consequence: removing fg is neither a sufficient fix nor a clean test of D1; the payoff has to be removed at the label (S1), not at the features.
- The standing **era-proxy audit** (`run_train_v5.era_proxy_audit`) is a Spearman rank-correlation of each channel against time. In run 2 it reported "none" truthfully — the fg barcode is periodic/oscillatory, not monotone, and the audit is structurally blind to that class of era identifier. In run 1 the same audit *did* flag eight volatility channels (ρ +0.21 … +0.29: `boll_width`, `Volatility_10/20/30`, `volatility_5d/20d`, `log_sigma_hat`, `intraday_range`) — on the 1962–2003 train era even the vol channels were monotone era proxies, reinforcing the overdetermination point.

### 3.3 Rank-IC of the saved best checkpoint

The saved `models/v5/member_*.pt` are the early-stopping *best* states, i.e. the step-10k models. Method: `member_0`, ~280-ticker even subsample (scaler refit on the subsample's train rows — the run's exact scaler was lost with the crashed finalize; robust pooled medians are stable at this subset size, and a fixed input perturbation applied identically to both eras has no mechanism to create a train-vs-val asymmetry — an era-symmetry argument, not a theorem about nonlinear models), 135–200 names per date, CPU `MambaRef` forward. Pooled over two probes (10 distinct train-era dates 2015–2023; 17 distinct val-era dates 2024-06 → 2024-11):

| era (dates) | 1d | 1w | 1m | 6m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| train (n=10) | +0.20 ± 0.06 | +0.28 ± 0.04 | +0.30 ± 0.03 | +0.43 ± 0.03 |
| val (n=17) | +0.05 ± 0.03 | +0.07 ± 0.03 | +0.11 ± 0.04 | +0.10 ± 0.05 |

Three findings, in decreasing order of certainty:

1. **Train-era memorization, growing with horizon.** Train IC is far beyond any genuine signal at 1w/1m/6m (reference: 0.02–0.05) and **increases with horizon** — +0.20 / +0.28 / +0.30 / +0.43, with 1d ≪ {1w, 1m} < 6m (the 1w → 1m step is within one standard error; the pattern is qualitative, not a fitted ∝ d_h law) — consistent with D2: the more times an outcome is revisited per epoch, the more it is memorized. Reproduced by five independent review probes (single train-date 6m ICs up to +0.67; two probes had 1w > 1m on individual dates, inside the same dispersion).
2. **Within-date IC measures D2, not D1.** A within-date rank correlation is insensitive to any per-date-constant score component by construction, so this table does not measure the per-date-shift memorization; D1's evidence is §3.1 + §3.2 + the CE trajectory of §2.
3. **The val column is suggestively positive at 1w/1m** — +0.07 ± 0.03 and +0.11 ± 0.04 (nominal t ≈ 2–2.6). Spacing honesty: the 17 pooled dates span ~175 calendar days (median adjacent gap ~11 days, minimum ~5), so the 1w figures are mildly overlap-correlated — not overlap-free — 1m more so, and 6m overlap-dominated and uninformative at 17 dates. This does not meet §6's evidential bar (one year, one member, substitute scaler, and per-date ICs disperse ±0.1–0.2 — one review probe's three lucky dates produced 1d ≈ +0.20, which did not survive a 12-date re-measurement at +0.03 ± 0.03), but it is consistent with the checkpoint carrying genuine mid-horizon ranking skill that raw val CE — the only metric the run ever computed — was structurally unable to register. That is D3(b) made concrete, and it is what S3's metrics exist to measure properly.

### 3.4 Onset is early and invariant to allocation; depth scales with revisiting

What the logs support, stated carefully (the degradation *onset* is left-censored below the first eval in both runs, and both first evals sit near 10k steps only because both runs evaluated at a ~10k cadence):

- In both runs the val optimum lies **below ~10k steps** — under 0.5 epoch (run 1) and under 0.11 epoch (run 2) — despite a 4.4× difference in training windows (5.26M → 23.1M) and a complete reallocation of the splits. Data quantity and split allocation do not move the failure.
- Run 2's train EMA crosses the marginal baseline within ~300 steps (~77k samples, each seen once) — the early train-CE gain cannot come from revisiting samples, so it is structure *shared across samples*. Its decomposition into date-level payoff versus genuinely transferable conditioning is not identifiable from the crossing alone; the date-level attribution rests on §3.1 (the payoff exists and is large) together with the net transfer failure (val CE never reaches the baseline).
- Degradation *depth* at the common ~10k-step mark differs sharply: run 1 (0.50 epoch) sat ≈ +0.6 nats above its (post-hoc) marginal, while run 2 (0.11 epoch) sat +0.009 above its logged baseline — deeper degradation at higher epoch count, consistent with D2's revisit-rate mechanism compounding on top of D1. Confound, named: run 1's val slice (2003–2013) sits up to a decade from its 1962–2003 train era while run 2's val year is adjacent to training, and distribution shift alone raises val CE at fixed conditioning quality — so this comparison *supports* D2 but cannot isolate it; D2's independent support is §3.3's horizon-monotone train IC.

### 3.5 Data-integrity census (bears on labels, backtest, and the fixes)

Full-universe census over the parquet shards (26,365,361 US rows, 16,367 sessions, 1962-01-02 → 2026-06-08):

- **Survivorship gradient.** Earliest last-trading-date among the 7,094 usable tickers is 2007-11-13; 0 usable tickers end before 2005, 18 before 2010, 243 before 2015, 1,148 before 2020 — while 2,102 end before the OOS start (which reconciles exactly with the run-2 log's `eval tickers: 4992` = 7,094 − 2,102). Recent eras are delisting-complete; **pre-~2008 training cross-sections consist almost entirely of eventual survivors**. Deep-history conditionals and any per-date statistic computed there are survivor-biased; val/OOS evaluation itself is honest.
- **Split-unadjusted prices confirmed by spot check**: AAPL close 499.23 → 129.04 across 2020-08-31 (4:1); NVDA 1208.88 → 121.79 across 2024-06-10 (10:1). The training-side label guard handles |log return| > ln 1.5; everything below that ratio (5:4, 4:3, 6:5 splits, large stock dividends) enters labels and features as fake returns.
- **The spike guard censors mostly-genuine tail outcomes.** Measured on 270–320-ticker subsamples with the repo's own functions (three independent probes): 0.07–0.12% of rows are |log return| > ln 1.5 prints, and only **15–38%** of them snap within 2% of a standard split ratio (the share depends on the ratio family assumed; under {3:2, 2, 5:2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 20} and their inverses it is ~24%) — the non-snapping majority are predominantly genuine extreme moves (or non-split data errors), currently masked silently. The mask removes roughly 0.1% / 0.3–0.5% / 1.1–1.8% / **5.4–7.3%** of 1d/1w/1m/6m has-future labels on the train era — and the rate is **era-skewed**: the val year runs ~1.3–2.3× the train-era rate at every horizon (6m val-year: 7.3–15.2% across five independent probes). Subsample dispersion dominates these rates — treat every figure in this bullet as order-of-magnitude, not a band — but the ordering (grows with horizon; larger in the val year) reproduced in every probe, so §3.1's val column, S1's val-date median pools, and S3's mask-filtered IC populations all inherit a larger, volatility-correlated hole in exactly the evaluation year. A tail-event exclusion of exactly the outcomes a long-only strategy monetizes or suffers, applied to training and validation via the same code path but not at the same rate.
- **Dividend-blind returns**: the feed carries raw close prints, which by definition exclude dividends; all labels and backtest returns are price returns, not total returns. After S1 this becomes a systematic cross-sectional tilt against high-yield names (their ex-dividend drops read as underperformance versus peers on those dates).
- **Calendar gaps**: 103 usable tickers contain an internal gap > 90 days, 65 > 365 days, 48 > 730 days. Horizons are counted in rows, so a "21-row" label across a multi-month halt (or a recycled symbol) is not a 1-month return, and windows spanning the gap carry no indicator of it.
- **Benchmark staleness**: the backtest's SPY reference CSV spans 2011-03-29 → 2026-04-23 while the OOS window runs to 2026-06-08 — the final ~32 sessions of the benchmark are a flat forward-fill. The SPY comparator is also price-only (dividend-blind on the benchmark side, flattering the strategy by roughly SPY's ~1–1.5%/yr yield).
- **Phantom sessions**: 42 weekend rows, all 1996 (26 Sundays + 16 Saturdays); 4 ancient single-name weekday rows (1976–1985); and — consequentially — **3 US-market holidays inside the current OOS tail** (2026-02-16 Presidents' Day with 2 names, 2026-04-03 Good Friday and 2026-05-25 Memorial Day with 1 each). These phantom sessions occupy slots in `global_date_bounds`' fixed 254-session tails and shift the cuts.
- **The OHLCV feed has no refresh path.** Nothing in the repository writes `TrainingData/ohlcv_parts` (the shards were committed once); `run_live_signals_v5.py --force-refresh` is an argparse stub ("Reserved hook"). The feed ends 2026-06-08; live scoring today silently scores ~6-week-stale windows, surfacing only a printed lag list.

## 4. Root-cause analysis

Three defects compound. None of them is "the model overfits; nothing to be done" — each is a specific design property with a specific correction.

**D1 — The objective rewards memorizing the calendar (dominant).**
The CE is computed against *absolute* z per ticker-day. Its largest learnable component on the training era is the per-date market shift (§3.1), and the inputs identify the date — redundantly (§3.2). Gradient descent therefore allocates capacity first to a date → market-outcome lookup: it lowers train CE across every ticker simultaneously (largest, most coherent gradient from step 0), transfers zero to future dates, and on validation produces confidently mis-centered conditionals — which is precisely the observed val-CE rise past the marginal point. Meanwhile both downstream consumers (`run_backtest_v4.py`, `run_live_signals_v5.py`) make decisions by ranking *across tickers within one day*. A per-date-constant shift leaves within-date *label* rankings (and any fixed monotone score of centered z) exactly invariant; for threshold probabilities such as P(up) the invariance is approximate — a common shift can reorder names whose predictive distributions have different widths and cross near the threshold — and the absolute `MIN_ACCEPTED` gates plus the backtest's cross-horizon argmax do shift with levels (the one-time threshold re-tune in S1 covers this). None of that changes the conclusion: the memorized per-date component is useless to the product and unknowable for the future; the objective spends the model on it anyway.

**D2 — Label/window redundancy makes an "epoch" ≈ many effective epochs.**
Stride-1 windows overlap in 251/252 rows; a d-day label overlaps its neighbors' paths for d−1 days. Each independent long-horizon outcome enters the loss ~d times per epoch (126× for 6m) under trivially perturbed inputs. Memorization of the 1m/6m outcome surfaces therefore outruns generalization long before one nominal epoch — the observed "best under 0.11 epoch" is mechanically expected, not anomalous. Two independent confirmations: §3.4's depth comparison (run 1 deeper at the same step count, with 4.4× the revisit rate (= the steps-per-epoch ratio 90,255/20,543, exact at any common step count)), and §3.3's train-IC ordering, monotone in d exactly as revisit-count scaling predicts.

**D3 — The measurement cannot see success where it might exist.**
(a) Run 2 evaluated every 10,000 steps (CLI override of the 2,000 default). The model passes the marginal-predictor point within the first few hundred steps (train EMA crosses the baseline between steps 200 and 300); the entire region where genuine generalization would first appear was never measured. "Best = first eval" is left-censored.
(b) The judged metric was raw CE. Under distribution shift, raw CE penalizes sharpness even when ranking skill is present — and §3.3's val column (1w/1m IC ≈ +0.07–0.11, suggestive though sub-threshold) is exactly the shape of skill raw CE cannot register.
(c) No per-horizon CE, no rank metric, no AUC was logged, although `final_architecture_v2.md` §Validation-metrics specifies AUC/ECE per horizon. The early-stop criterion is not the quantity the product consumes. No ensemble-level metric exists anywhere (see §2's caveat).

**Assessment of the fixes already in `7979e41` (committed, never run):**
- Plateau LR decay ("Defect-1 fix"): addresses a symptom. The executed runs had effectively constant LR; the schedule did not cause the rise, and decay cannot prevent it — the first evals already bracket the optimum. Harmless; keep.
- In-loop calibrated judging `judged_val_ce` ("Defect-2 fix"): the *idea* is right (temperature fit and scoring on disjoint halves removes the global-sharpness penalty; T=1 on the grid makes the fit-half selection no worse than raw, with no strict guarantee on the disjoint score half — negligible at these sample sizes). Three implementation defects found in review, all verified: (i) the "evenly-strided" calibration subsample truncates to the **head of a ticker-ordered fold** whenever the floor-stride under-covers — coverage `= cal_cap · (n // cal_cap) / n < 1` for any fold size that is not an exact multiple, worst in `cal_cap ≤ n < 2·cal_cap`, which is exactly the recommended defaults (150k fold, 100k cap → stride 1 → the first 100k rows, deterministically excluding the alphabetically-last third of val tickers; counterfactually, run 2's 1M/100k override values *would have* yielded an even stride of 10 — run 2's code predates this function and never executed it). Fix: ceil-stride or `linspace` indexing. (ii) The logged BEAT/MISS verdict compares `vce` (scored on the odd-parity half of the capped subset) against a `val_base` computed once on the full fold. (iii) The logged `raw` CE is computed on the capped subset while `val_base` and the `--no-calibrate-eval` path use the full fold. Row-set differences of order 0.003–0.007 nats — the same order as the observed miss margins — so align all three on one row set (the score half), or print each number's row count. With these fixed, keep as a secondary metric — a scalar per-horizon temperature still cannot repair per-date mis-centering. One more commensurability hazard while editing here: the class-term weight λ_cls = 0.1 exists as independent duplicated defaults in `v5_loss` and `val_marginal_baseline_ce` — hoist it into `V5Config` and thread it to both, or a future edit to one side silently breaks every BEAT/MISS comparison at the 0.006-nat margin scale.

**Explicitly ruled out** (each checked against code and logs):
- Mechanical train/eval asymmetry — none: identical sample construction and masking on both paths; `evaluate_ce` uses eval mode and mask-weighted averaging; conditioning dropout is inactive when slots are `None`; no batch-dependent normalization exists in the architecture.
- Input-side leakage — none found: all 40 features causal at the anchor; labels embargoed to their split (`label_mask`); scaler fit on train rows only.
- Data quality as the *driver* — the spike guard, EWMA exclusion, and fp16 round-trip behave as specified; the integrity issues in §3.5 add noise/bias but none of them produces the observed early-onset, allocation-invariant collapse.
- Split allocation / data quantity — §3.4.

## 5. Corrective design

Ordered by leverage. S1–S3 are the substance; S4 is protocol; S5 is the backtest-side counterpart; S6 is hygiene.

### S1. Cross-sectionally center the labels (removes D1's dominant payoff)

Definition: for every (date `t`, horizon `h`), over all tickers whose label passes the **full** mask criteria — finite `z`, spike-free, **and within the date's split boundary** (`target_dates ≤ split_end` of the date's time bucket; every row at one date shares one time bucket, so this is coherent) — compute the cross-sectional median `c[t,h]` and train on

```
z'[i,t,h] = z[i,t,h] − c[t,h]
```

- **Median**, not mean: robust to residual unguarded split prints and fat tails.
- **Why the embargo term is in the pool criteria**: without it, a calendar-gap ticker whose row-counted d_h target lands beyond the split boundary would contribute post-boundary prices to a median that is subtracted into *trained* labels — an ε-magnitude but formally real embargo violation (a median over ≥ 50 names moves only on a rank flip, but the fix costs nothing).
- **Minimum cross-section**: if fewer than `K = 50` tickers are eligible at (t,h), mask those labels (`m = 0`) rather than leaving them uncentered — a mixed definition would be incoherent. `K` scales down for small universes — `K = min(50, ceil(universe/2))` — or a hard K = 50 makes every `--smoke`/dev run (≤ 6 tickers) mask everything and exit at "no training windows". Census: 2,980 of 16,367 sessions have < 50 names — 18% of *sessions* (nearly all sessions through 1973-05, plus 49 later dates) but ≲ 1% of *windows*, so the cost is negligible. Pool population: labels that are *enumerable as samples* — i.e. the mask criteria **plus the anchor floor** (row index ≥ 125), matching the S4(c) probe fix; listing-era rows that can never train should not vote in the median. Dependency: if S6's calendar-gap mask is adopted into `label_mask`, the pool criteria inherit it automatically (the pool is defined as mask-passing labels) — implement the gap mask first or simultaneously so gap-ticker pseudo-returns never enter `c[t,h]`.
- **Ticker-holdout composition**: under `eval_mode ∈ {ticker, both}`, compute `c` from non-eval tickers only, apply to all (same discipline as the scaler; strictly unnecessary — labels are targets, not fitted inputs — but it costs nothing).
- **Survivorship interaction, named**: pre-~2008 cross-sections are survivors-only (§3.5), so deep-history `c[t,h]` is a *survivor-pool* median — the centered target means "relative to eventual survivors" in the early eras and "relative to the full universe" later; the target's definition drifts across eras. S6's per-sub-era metric reporting surfaces the symptom; the clean resolution once centering lands is S6's training-start ablation (~2008–2010).
- **Leakage**: with the pool criteria above, none: `c[t,h]` uses only prices the surviving labels at that date may themselves use. Inputs are untouched; target dates do not change.
- **What this closes and what it does not**: centering zeroes the per-date *location* channel — the measured 15–21% of label variance (§3.1) that constitutes D1's dominant payoff. Date-keyed structure that survives centering: (a) per-date cross-sectional *dispersion/shape* — the model predicts a full histogram, so memorizing which train dates were high-dispersion still pays CE through predicted width; (b) a loading-heterogeneity residual — the market move enters `z_i` scaled ≈ β_i/σ_i, which varies across tickers, so the common component is not exactly additive and atypical-β/σ names retain a date-keyed residual; (c) sector-date structure (the feed carries no sector field, so sector-centering is unavailable). Strictly stronger option for (a), with identical leakage character: per-date robust *scale* normalization, `z'' = (z − median_t) / max(1.4826·MAD_t, floor)` — the 1.4826 normal-consistency factor matters: dividing by raw MAD dilates z″ by ≈ 1.48 for near-Gaussian dates, pushing ~1% of labels into the HL-Gauss clamp region and silently shrinking θ from 0.674 to ~0.45 σ-units, beyond what the threshold re-tune covers — a per-date monotone affine map (the floor, e.g. 0.25, guards the degenerate low-dispersion date whose unfloored MAD would explode z'' past the bin range), so within-date label rankings and consumer semantics are preserved exactly as for centering; it costs one more one-time threshold re-tune. Plan: ship centering; adopt MAD-scaling if S3's metrics show a residual date-keyed width payoff (calibrated-CE improving while IC stagnates). Residual per-ticker-era idiosyncratic memorization remains an ordinary overfitting channel, handled by S2/S4.
- **Consumer compatibility**: within-date, within-horizon label rankings are invariant (see D1 for the threshold-probability caveat). Semantics that change: the histogram models the *market-relative* return distribution; `P(up)` becomes `P(return exceeds the date median by θ·σ̂·√d_h)`; `Pred_Q*` become relative quantiles. One forfeited capability, stated rather than implied away: post-centering, no output can express "nothing attractive in absolute terms — hold cash", so the gated strategy becomes structurally near-always-invested in the best market-relative name; per D1 the removed absolute component was unlearnable anyway, so nothing real is lost, but the absolute-exposure decision now lives outside the model (position sizing / an external regime rule), by design. Ship as **one re-tune package with S1**: the absolute gates (`MIN_ACCEPTED` in `run_backtest_v4.py`, `--min-accepted` in `run_live_signals_v5.py`) re-tuned as relative-conviction floors; per-horizon score standardization or per-horizon thresholds in both consumers — the cross-horizon argmax currently favors the horizon with the fattest marginal up-rate (0.21 at 1d vs 0.26–0.31 at 6m in the logs), and S2 will additionally make 6m the least-trained head; and **cap or exclude 6m from the consumers' argmax** until the multi-year rolling-origin evidence of §6 exists, since §6 itself shows single-year 6m validation is statistically vacuous. Record the centering definition and `K` in `v5_meta.json`.
- **Class-rate probe**: S1 relies on the class-rate log to surface the neutral-ward rate shift, but as coded `class_rate_log` (a) runs only under `eval_mode == "time"`, (b) never applies `label_mask` — it buckets every finite z including spike-censored and embargo-crossing labels training never sees (a 6m divergence of at least the §3.5 spike-censoring share plus the ≈2.4% embargo-crossing share (569k of 23.7M train-era 6m has-future labels — labels concentrate in the name-dense recent sessions, ~3× the naive 126/15,859 session-share; for the val bucket the crossing share at 6m is ~50%) — strictly larger than §3.5's spike-only figure — volatility-correlated) — and (c) internally hardcodes `row_splits(f, bounds, "time", False, False)`, so merely un-gating it under `both` would still fold held-out tickers' never-trained rows into the reported train/val rates. Fix all three: run it for `time` and `both`, apply the same mask (or have S1's masking NaN the affected z so the probe's NaN-exclusion inherits it), and thread the real `eval_mode`/`eval_tickers` into its `row_splits` call.
- **Geometry**: z variance shrinks 15–21%; the 60-bin ±4.044 range and `theta_bins` remain valid. `val_marginal_baseline_ce` needs no code change — it reads whatever labels the dataset carries — but its value will drop (entropy of a narrower marginal): compare only within-run.
- **Implementation locus**: `run_train_v5.main`, after `global_date_bounds` and `assign_eval_tickers` (the pool criteria need `bounds` and, under ticker holdout, `eval_tickers` — "right after `build_frames`" would place it where its inputs don't exist), and before `class_rate_log` and `build_memmap_and_samples`. Harden `label_mask` with an `isfinite(z)` term in the same change: today `v5_loss` maps a NaN label to a full-weight z = 0 target (`torch.nan_to_num`), and while probes found zero mask-positive non-finite labels among enumerated samples (the live paths — pre-EWMA-seed rows below the anchor floor at row 125, and the hypothetical non-positive close, of which the feed contains zero — are both empirically vacuous), S1's K-masking should be structurally safe rather than convention-safe. No architecture change. ### S2. Thin the redundant long-horizon labels (slows D2)

Keep every anchor's 1d/1w label; include the 21d label only when `(date_ordinal(a) + offset_h(ticker)) % 4 == 0` and the 126d label only when `(date_ordinal(a) + offset_h(ticker)) % 25 == 0`, i.e. stride ≈ `d_h / 5`. Key the phase to the **calendar-day ordinal (days since epoch)** — explicitly not a session index, which a backfill would shift just like a row index — and not the per-ticker row index — a row-index phase silently re-selects every ticker's surviving 1m/6m label subset whenever a historical backfill/restatement shifts row counts (exactly what an adjusted-close re-source would do), while the date key is refresh-invariant with identical de-synchronization. `offset_h(ticker)` must come from a **stable hash** (sha256, as `assign_eval_tickers` already does) — never Python's builtin `hash()`, whose per-process randomization (PYTHONHASHSEED) would desynchronize the per-GPU dataset builds and destroy member reproducibility. The per-ticker offset de-synchronizes the surviving 6m cross-section across calendar dates. Order of operations: compute z → center (S1) → thin the train mask. Applied to **train masks only**; validation and eval masks stay complete. (Mask-monotonicity means thinning can never orphan an anchor: any anchor with a valid 6m label also has valid shorter-horizon labels.)

- Effect: each independent long-horizon outcome contributes O(5) rather than O(d) times per epoch; the memorization clock for the 1m/6m heads slows ~4× / ~25× while every window still trains the short-horizon heads (no compute is wasted). §3.3's train-IC-vs-horizon monotonicity is the direct measurement of the mechanism this targets.
- Side effect (intended): the masked-mean loss reweights toward short horizons, approximately equalizing the *independent-information* rate across {1w, 1m, 6m} (≈ 5 label copies per independent outcome each; 1d stays at 1 copy — exact four-way equalization is precisely the `∝ 1/d_h` weighting alternative below) instead of the raw label count. Alternative with the same intent and less variance reduction — per-horizon loss weights `∝ 1/d_h` — is acceptable if the reweighting is preferred smooth; do not do both.

- Extend the validation bucket in `build_memmap_and_samples` to carry `(ticker_id, date, target_dates)` per sample — the anchor date and the per-horizon N×4 target dates; the membership rule and the mask-filtered metrics need the *stored* target dates, since reconstructing them as anchor + d_h sessions on a global calendar is wrong for the 103 gap tickers whose horizons are row-counted (two int arrays; required for any per-date metric; `_subsample_bucket` is key-agnostic and carries them through).

- **Stopping/gating separation (selection-bias control).** One **shared partition for all horizons**: the val year's 254 sessions split into four contiguous ~63-session blocks, alternating stopping/gating (2 + 2 per year), with the phase **pre-registered** (block 1 = stopping). Membership is decided by **label target dates, not purge windows**: every stopping-side quantity — the stopping ICs, the calibrated-CE tie-breaker, and the temperature fit — is computed only on labels whose `target_dates` fall inside stopping blocks, and every gating quantity only on labels whose target dates fall inside gating blocks (one comparison against the already-stored per-label target dates; exact per horizon, no purge-width arithmetic, no cap, and it assigns each label wholly to one side and closes the *terminal-date* channel — but a label is a sum of daily returns over (anchor, target], so stopping-side labels anchored near seams still carry return-path segments realized inside gating blocks). The **stopping blocks** drive early stopping and the plateau-LR coupling; the **gating blocks** are touched only by §6's gates. Why one coarse partition: fine interleaves fail — alternating weeks share every 21-session label path across halves, and separate per-horizon partitions assign conflicting roles to the same dates. Consequences: {1d, 1w} gating keeps ~127 sessions/year (1w Neff ≈ 25); **1m is adjudicated pooled-only** on the 2 gating blocks/year (≈ 6 clean 1m observations/year, ≈ 18–24 pooled — weak, and stated as such in §6); the target-date rule induces a {1d, 1w} stopping fold of roughly 116–126 sessions/year depending on phase and membership reading (power conclusions unchanged); 6m fits nowhere in a 254-session year and stays report-only. Scoping consequence: because return paths straddle seams, the stopping-side **calibrated-CE tie-breaker and the temperature fit use {1d, 1w} stopping-side labels under interval membership** (anchor *and* target inside stopping blocks); 1m/6m are excluded from those stopping-side quantities, consistent with their eviction from stopping everywhere else. ### S5. Fix the backtest's realized-return, lifecycle, cost, and reference handling

Verified defects in `run_backtest_v4.py`, and one caution on the fix:

- **Split prints enter P&L, and the guard itself is look-ahead universe selection.** Realized returns and mark-to-market use raw closes, guarded only by `SPIKE_THRESHOLD = 0.80` on |pct_change| with whole-ticker rejection: a 2:1 forward split (−50% print) **passes** and books as a fake ~−69% log-return loss; any reverse split (≥ +100%) rejects the entire ticker. Worse, the rejection is computed once over each ticker's *full OOS series before the daily loop*, so every day's candidate set is conditioned on future prints — and because reverse splits typically follow price collapses, future losers are preferentially deleted from the pool, a directional look-ahead bias on top of the fake-loss booking.
- **Do not fix it with a blanket ln(1.5) exclusion.** §3.5 shows the majority of >ln(1.5) prints are genuine moves; excluding every such interval would delete real crashes from P&L — an upward bias a deployed strategy cannot replicate. Correct fix: a ratio-snap detector (print within ~2% of a standard split ratio, corroborated by the reciprocal volume jump) that *adjusts or excludes detected splits only*, plus a log line counting exclusions; genuine tail moves stay in P&L. The detector and the realized-return bookkeeping must both be **date-gap-aware**: `pct_change` runs over each ticker's consecutive rows, so a genuine multi-week move across a data gap (48 usable tickers have >730-day gaps; halts continue into the OOS year) currently reads as a single-day print — falsely whole-ticker-rejecting above the threshold, or booking a multi-session move as one day below it — and a naive detector would inherit the same blindness; compare each print against its actual calendar gap. Contract note: the backtest currently reads only `Date`/`Close`/probability columns and the forecast CSVs carry no volume, so volume corroboration requires a small cross-stage change — add a `Volume` column to the forecast CSVs (additive, backward-compatible) or have the backtest read the parquet shards directly.
- **Booking is misaligned with the exit calendar — delisting is the worst case, not the only one.** `Actual_LogR` is `log(Close.shift(-days)/Close)` on the *ticker's own* row calendar, while exits are scheduled on the *union* calendar (`exit_idx = min(i + Days, len(common_dates) − 1)`): any held ticker missing union sessions books a return spanning price movement beyond its exit date; when the ticker's data ends mid-hold (2,102 usable tickers die before the OOS start; deaths continue through the OOS year), the equity freezes at the last print and the realized return books as NaN — *excluded from the success count*; and a horizon running past the OOS window end is exit-clamped to the final session while likewise booking NaN. Single fix covering all three: book every trade from its entry close to its **exit-date mark-to-market close** (last available print if the ticker died, with the loss booked), so the trade log and the equity path are one accounting. Residual bias, made visible rather than fixed: the feed carries no delisting-reason or terminal-value field, so a bankruptcy-style delisting still books at its last print, above true recovery — add a configurable delisting haircut parameter and a forced-exit counter to the trade log so the sensitivity is explicit. (Cosmetic but trap-laying for whoever does this rewrite: the rejection banner prints "spikes (>30%)" while the threshold is 0.80.)
- **Execution timing — and its consequence for the 1d head.** The backtest fills at the same close the signal is computed from (features are causal through close(t); entry books ln(C[t+d]/C[t])), which live operation cannot do — the live scorer's default `as_of = today − 1 BDay` means real fills happen no earlier than the next session's close. Fixing the fill to t+1 exposes a structural problem: the trained 1d label covers (t, t+1], which has **zero overlap** with the earliest honest holding interval (t+1, t+2] — the corrected backtest would score the 1d head on a quantity it was never trained to predict (expected ≈ 0 even for a perfect model), while 1w/1m/6m retain 4/5, 20/21, 125/126 interval overlap. Two coherent resolutions: **re-anchor all labels to next-close entry** — `z = ln(C[t+1+d]/C[t+1]) / (σ̂[t]·√d)`, with the spike-free window and `target_dates` shifted accordingly to `(t+1, t+1+d]` and `dates[t+1+d]` (a split print at t+1 cancels out of the re-anchored ratio, so the unshifted window would over-censor; a print at t+1+d would otherwise be missed) — the standard skip-a-day convention (a small `_targets` change; 1w/1m/6m are barely moved and 1d becomes genuinely tradable); or exclude 1d from the consumers' tradable argmax (alongside the 6m cap) and keep it as a diagnostic head — in which case **also drop 1d from S3's stopping combination**, or checkpoint selection puts half its weight on a head the product cannot use. Re-anchoring is recommended: it aligns every horizon's label with an executable interval once and for all. Propagation checklist for the +1 shift: `_embargo_dates`' last-valid-anchor arithmetic becomes `pos − (d+1)`, and §6's session-count formulas become `253 − d` — negligible everywhere except the 6m bookkeeping, but the embargo-audit artifact is consumer-read and must not silently disagree with the labels.
- **Transaction costs are zero.** The impact is turnover-dependent: the strategy *structure* permits at most one round-trip per two sessions (exit-day re-entry is blocked), i.e. ~126 round-trips/yr, which at 10–20 bp per round-trip bounds the omission at ~13–25%/yr in a 1d-dominant regime; the only extant trade log (`trade_summary_prob_strategy.csv`, v4-era: 17 trades spanning 2023-04 → 2025-10, ~30 months) implies ~0.7–1.4%/yr at its historical mix. Either way it is unmodeled and biased upward. Add a per-trade cost parameter and report P&L at 0 / 10 / 25 bp, on the tradability-filtered candidate set (S3) — cost assumptions are meaningless for the sub-dollar illiquid tail an unfiltered argmax over ~5k names systematically selects.
- **The random baseline is not a valid significance reference.** It is always-invested in daily 1d picks (the strategy holds 1–126 days and sits in cash below the gate), samples only non-NaN next-day returns (so it never experiences the delisting freeze — an asymmetry that *inverts against the strategy* once the forced-exit fix lands), `random.choice(returns_by_date[date]*5)` replicates the list five times to no statistical effect, and 50 runs make the z-score coarse. Fix: a protocol-matched null — random candidate from the day's *gated* candidate set, same horizon-hold, cash, and exit-day re-entry-blackout rules as the strategy, forced-exit outcomes included — with ≥ 1,000 runs.
- **Benchmark**: refresh the SPY feed (flat-ffilled for the last ~32 OOS sessions) and use total-return SPY (the price-only comparator omits ~1–1.5%/yr of dividends, flattering the strategy).
- **Status**: even with all of the above, one 254-session single-position path over winner's-curse-selected trades cannot meet §6's evidential standard in either direction — the backtest is *descriptive*; §6's IC/CE gates alone adjudicate.

### S6. Hygiene and data-integrity items (non-causal for the collapse; fix opportunistically, ordered by expected value)

- **Replace the blanket spike guard with the split detector on the label side too** (§3.5): the ln(1.5) mask both misses sub-1.5 splits *and* censors a mostly-genuine majority of extreme moves — ~5.4–6.5% of 6m labels, volatility-correlated, from training and validation alike. With a ratio-snap + volume-corroborated detector, detected splits are masked (or prices adjusted) while genuine tails stay — the HL-Gauss z-clamp already handles them — and the histogram head finally trains on the tail events the strategy will face. The same detector must also gate the **EWMA exclusion** in `ewma_sigma_hat`: today every |r| > ln 1.5 return is excluded from the variance update, so after a *genuine* crash σ̂ is understated and the following weeks' z inflated — post-detector, only detected splits skip the update while genuine tails feed it. Until then, log the per-horizon censorship rate at every run, and report the §6 gate ICs and the top-decile realized-z additionally on a gap-aware *uncensored* outcome set as a sensitivity — the gates otherwise adjudicate on an outcome population missing ~5–12% of exactly the volatility-correlated 6m tail the strategy monetizes or suffers, while the P&L faces it.
- **OHLCV refresh path** (§3.5): none exists — nothing writes the parquet shards and `--force-refresh` is a stub, so live scoring degrades monotonically with feed age (six weeks stale at the time of writing). Implement the incremental tail fetch, or make the live scorer hard-fail (not merely print) beyond a staleness bound.
- **Survivorship control** (§3.5): pre-~2008 training data is survivor-only. Cheapest guard: report S3 metrics per train sub-era; optionally ablate training start at ~2008–2010; log per-year universe death rates so the gradient is visible.
- **Dividend-blind labels** (§3.5): price returns, not total returns; post-S1 this tilts the cross-sectional target against high-yield names around ex-dates. Fix by sourcing adjusted/total-return closes for labels (features can stay raw); otherwise acknowledge the bias.

- **Phantom-session filter — at data load, from a full-universe census, with a small threshold and a pass-through for new dates**: filtering `global_date_bounds` alone fixes the cut arithmetic but leaves the phantom rows everywhere else — as training rows/labels/window content, and as 1–2-candidate "trading days" in the backtest, whose calendar is built from forecast-CSV dates. A `weekday > 4` filter alone would miss all the holidays.
- **Forecast surface is future-conditioned**: `build_memmap_and_samples` skips any anchor with `mask.sum() == 0` *before* the eval-surface append, so each ticker's final session (the one anchor with no label at any horizon — the most recent, most decision-relevant date) and anchors whose entire forward path is spike-masked get no forecast row — future information selecting the evaluation universe. ## 6. Decision rule for the next run

Statistical form: per-date ICs at horizon d are serially correlated because adjacent dates share d−1 days of label path, and the embargo leaves only 254−d val sessions with horizon-d labels; the effective number of independent observations is therefore ≈ `(254 − d) / d` per horizon — ≈ 253 (1d), ≈ 50 (1w), ≈ 11 (1m), ≈ **1** (6m) for a full year, and **half that on the gating half** every gate actually reads. Naive per-date standard errors are anticonservative at the longer horizons. Compute IC t-statistics with a Newey–West/HAC standard error (`lag = max(5, d_h − 1)`, S3's single definition), a block bootstrap over date blocks, or on a d_h-strided date subset; treat the 6m gate as unpowered on a single year (report it, never gate on it — including inside the S3 stopping metric).

Multiple-looks discipline: each gate is evaluated **once per member, at the early-stop-selected checkpoint** (and once for the finalize ensemble) — not "at any point in training", which at a 1k-step cadence × 4 members would be hundreds of looks and would inflate a nominal 3σ gate to a few-percent familywise false-success rate. A qualifying result additionally needs persistence — checked on the **stopping-block metric** at the evals neighboring the selected checkpoint (that metric is computed at every eval anyway, so persistence adds zero looks at the gating blocks) rather than qualifying on a single spike. The CE gate gets the same treatment as the IC gate: judge it as a paired per-date (model − baseline) CE difference with a HAC/bootstrap standard error at the selected checkpoint, not as a raw scan of point values whose decisive margins are millinats.

One-shot OOS discipline: the last-254-session OOS window is consumed a little every time a design decision is checked against it; across S1–S6 iterations it degrades from test set toward validation set. Backtest/OOS results are examined once per *design freeze*, and the OOS tail should roll forward onto newly accrued data at each iteration (the feed refresh in S6 enables this). At each freeze, that one-shot read includes **the full S3 metric suite on the OOS tail** (mask-filtered, HAC-corrected, tradability-stratified, top-of-ranking) reported beside the descriptive backtest — it is the only statistically meaningful estimate fully outside the stopping/gating design loop, since even the gating halves belong to val years whose measurements informed this document's own design. On that last point, stated plainly: the primary val year (2024-06 → 2025-06) is **design-contaminated** — §3.1's val column, §3.3's val-date IC probes, and §3.5's val-year censorship rates all shaped S1–S6 and the gate placement — so the single-year gate on it is doubly provisional; the three rolling-origin years are clean of val-*targeted* probing — only aggregate train-era statistics (§3.1's train column, §3.3's train-date panel) touched their calendar spans, a far weaker contact than the primary year's direct measurement, but not literally zero — and they carry the pooled verdicts' weight; as a sensitivity, the pooled success verdict is also reported on the three rolling years alone, excluding the contaminated primary.

Power reality check, which shapes the rule — computed on the **gating halves** the gates actually read (under target-date membership the ~127 gating-block target-sessions per year give ≈ 25 effective observations at 1w): at per-date IC dispersion ±0.1–0.2, a single-year |t| ≥ 3 requires mean IC ≈ 0.06–0.12 at 1w — **well above** the realistic-signal band (0.02–0.05) this document itself expects; pooled over 3–4 years of gating halves (Neff ≈ 75–100 at 1w) the detectable IC is ≈ 0.032–0.069, reaching only the upper part of the realistic band. 1m is adjudicated **only pooled, on the shared partition's 2 gating blocks per year** (≈ 6 clean 1m observations per year, ≈ 18–24 pooled → pooled detectable IC ≈ 0.061–0.14 — a weak gate, honestly stated; full tails are never used because they include stopping-block dates, violating the success-gates-on-clean-blocks rule below); 6m is reported only. A single-year 3σ gate therefore cannot detect the most probable true-signal outcome, and even the pooled gate is honest only about medium-or-better signal. One asymmetry recovers power where the bias direction permits it: winner's curse inflates the selected checkpoint's *stopping-half* statistics, so a **failure** reading taken on the *full* pooled years (both halves) is evaluated through an upward-biased lens and is thereby conservative — the failure gate may use full years (Neff ≈ 150–200 at 1w), while success gates must stay on the clean gating halves. The calibrated-CE route is weaker still for pure ranking skill: IC ρ ∈ [0.02, 0.05] is worth `−0.5·ln(1−ρ²)` ≈ 2e-4–1.3e-3 nats — at or below the baseline-flavor (~1e-4–1e-3), row-set (~0.003–0.007), and baseline-lower-bound (the logged constant sums two independently-optimal terms no single histogram attains, ~1e-5–1e-4) tolerances quantified in §1/§4 — so CE is a secondary diagnostic, never the adjudicator, and any CE claim is judged as a paired per-date (model − baseline) difference with a HAC/bootstrap standard error at the selected checkpoint.

Step zero, before any implementation: **measure the existing run-2 ensemble**. Score `models/v5/member_{0..3}.pt` and their 4-member mean over the full 254-session val year with the S3 suite (both scores, trivial comparators, top-decile realized z, per-date IC dispersion) — ~2 GPU-minutes per member on the training box. This settles §2's never-measured ensemble-vs-baseline caveat, replaces §3.3's 17-date val column with Neff ≈ 50 estimates, and **pre-registers the measured per-date IC dispersions** (unfiltered and tradability-filtered) that every power figure below currently assumes at ±0.1–0.2 from small probe cross-sections — the sampling component alone shrinks 2–6× at 590–5,000 names, so the true dispersions must be measured, not assumed. It touches no OOS data.

Pre-registered rule. The rolling-origin retrains are **unconditional** — they are the adjudicating dataset, run regardless of the first year's outcome (a conditional trigger would leave the likeliest null outcome, single-year t < 1.5, with no defined path and no pooled data to judge it by). Their truncation dates are **pre-registered to tile disjointly** — under the two-tail geometry (val tail = sessions [D−508, D−254) of data-end D), the set is pre-registered as **session offsets, not calendar dates** — data-end_k = the session exactly k·254 sessions before the primary data-end (k = 1, 2, 3), which makes the tiling exact by construction (calendar-month truncations drift by a few sessions per seam, since a year is 250–253 sessions); the offsets are defined on the **phantom-filtered session calendar with the date-count census artifact frozen first** — adopting the S6 whitelist after pre-registration would shift every k·254 seam by up to 3 sessions — giving val tails ≈ 2021-06→2022-06, 2022-06→2023-06, 2023-06→2024-06, and the primary 2024-06→2025-06: a clean partition of mid-2021 → mid-2025. (An earlier draft's example was off by one tail; and a casual year-end truncation would overlap the primary val year by ~6 months, double-counting dates in the pooled gates — same realized outcomes scored by models trained on nearly-nested data.) They require one small trainer change, listed here as a prerequisite: a `--data-end` argument that truncates the loaded dates, since `global_date_bounds` anchors the fixed tails to the maximum date on disk and no truncation flag exists today. **The gating object is the finalize ensemble** (the shipped object — pre-registered to remove the ~5-semi-correlated-chances ambiguity of gating members individually); per-member gate values are reported as supporting evidence. All gates are evaluated on each run's **gating date-half** (S3's separation), on the consumer-aligned score, and — for any "exploitable" verdict — must also hold on the tradability-filtered subset and be confirmed by the top-of-ranking metric (S3). Pooling across years additionally reports a year-clustered standard error next to the pooled HAC t and requires, for success, that **no per-year mean IC falls below −1 per-year standard error** — 3–4 years are too few clusters to absorb a regime-driven year effect, so a pooled 3σ carried by one anomalous year does not qualify; strict all-years-positive sign-consistency was softened after a power computation showed it costs 16–20% false negatives at exactly the pooled detectable edge (P(all 4 positive | true IC at the edge) ≈ 0.80–0.84) while the −1-SE form blocks the anomalous-year pathology at ~2% cost:

- Horizon scope of the gates, with the rationale stated: 1w and 1m carry the standard gates; **1d is gated only cost-conditioned** — it is the most-powered horizon (Neff ≈ 253/yr) and becomes tradable under S5's re-anchoring, but a 1d-dominant strategy pays ~13–25%/yr in round-trip costs, so a 1d success claim requires the top-decile realized-z to clear the S5 cost model at 10–25 bp, not a bare IC t-statistic; a cost-cleared 1d gate counts toward success, and the failure verdict below explicitly covers only cost-adjusted 1d alongside 1w/1m.
- **Strong single-year success** — gating-block t ≥ 3 at 1w on the first run (attainable only if IC ≈ 0.06–0.12, the same Neff ≈ 25 figure as the power paragraph): continue the line immediately, with the rolling-origin runs proceeding in parallel as confirmation before any deployment-scale conclusion.
- **Pooled success** (the expected shape if medium-or-better signal exists) — pooled across the 3–4 val years, overlap-corrected |t| ≥ 3 with the per-year floor above, at 1w (pooled gating halves, detectable IC ≈ 0.032–0.069) or at 1m (pooled gating blocks of its own 63-session partition — never full tails, which contain stopping-half dates): continue the line.
- **Failure, stated as the exclusion bound it actually is** — full-year pooled |t| < ~1.5 at both 1w and 1m (conservative per the asymmetry above) and centered val CE never beating the centered baseline in any year. This **rules out IC ≳ 0.03–0.05 at ~2σ**; it does *not* exclude a true IC ≈ 0.02, which would land here with meaningful probability — so the verdict reads "no signal of exploitable size demonstrated", not "no signal exists". Still *valid evidence* — unlike the two runs to date, which never tested the question — and it moves the discussion to features/horizons, not training mechanics; the trivial-comparator ICs (S3) then separate feature-set-dead from pipeline-dead.
- **Residual band** — pooled t ∈ [1.5, 3): genuinely ambiguous after four years of evidence; treated as "insufficient signal at acceptable confidence", i.e. the failure path decision-wise, but documented separately since it licenses cheap follow-ups (feature ablations, horizon changes) that a clean failure would not.

Single-year verdicts in either direction are provisional by construction; the pooled gates adjudicate.

Additional expectation-setting: train IC will still exceed val IC (residual per-ticker-era memorization survives S1); the gap should shrink from the current ~3–10× (§3.3) to low single digits at the early-stop point. A realistic good outcome is val IC in the 0.02–0.05 band on 1w/1m — §3.3's suggestive +0.07–0.11 at 1w/1m on the discarded checkpoint marks the upper end of what might be recoverable, and small numbers are the normal scale of real signal in this domain.
